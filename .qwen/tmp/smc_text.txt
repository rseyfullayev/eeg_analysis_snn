Abstract
We derive, from a latent-variable generative model over EEG window embeddings, a single
discriminative loss whose terms are exactly the assembled Phase-1A/1B objective of SWEEP-
Net: decoupled contrastive learning (DCL), Sinkhorn-infused positive matching, class-conditional
unbalanced optimal transport, per-class binary-divergence sparsity, and nullspace subject pro-
jection.
The derivation follows the VAE template—introduce a variational posterior, apply
Jensen, obtain a bound—but the latent model serves purely as scaffolding; no reconstruction
is performed.
We prove that optimizing the resulting loss is, by construction, a solution to
subject-ID memorization (the emotion prototype carries no subject index; subject signal is con-
fined to a low-rank subspace that is projected out), and that Phase 1B’s classification readout is
the deterministic sufficient statistic of the same model. Analytic certification of well-posedness,
boundedness, definiteness, non-degeneracy, and the admissible range of the entropic regulariser
ε is provided in the accompanying real-analysis appendix (Track B).
1
Introduction
1.1
Two Pathologies
Cross-subject EEG emotion recognition on the SEED-V 5-class benchmark is afflicted by two coupled
failures:
(i) Subject-ID memorisation (“Spatial Skull” + “Temporal Tempo”). The encoder fits skull
geometry, electrode impedance, and individual alpha frequency (IAF) instead of affective con-
tent. A linear subject probe already cuts from 91.35% to 64.10% after an earlier alignment
attempt, yet cross-subject accuracy plateaus at 29–32%.
(ii) Gaslighting (temporal label pollution). A 60 s Fear/Disgust trial contains ∼5–10 s of
genuine affective burst buried in ∼50 s of baseline. Window-level global labels entangle baseline
with burst.
1.2
Known-Dead Configurations
The following approaches have been tried and are provably or empirically dead; the derivation must
not reduce to any of them:
• cMMD (class-conditional MMD, two movable distributions, no anchor) →constant-vector
collapse.
• DANN / GRL (min–max saddle) →NaN blowup (7 × 1015 gradient norm).
• Balanced OT for alignment →SCDA subject-mining pathology (87% subject probe).
• Back-prop through the Sinkhorn fixed point; queue-vs-queue OT (zero-gradient path).
1.3
The VAE Template
The VAE derivation proceeds: want log p(x); face an intractable marginal; introduce a variational
posterior; apply Jensen; obtain the ELBO; optimise it. We do the same. We are not generating or
reconstructing EEG. The latent model is scaffolding to derive the discriminative loss.
1.4
Payoff
The final loss is our current objective, and the derivation proves:
(a) The emotion term has no subject index ⇒memorisation is unrewarded.
(b) Subject signal is confined to a low-rank term that is projected out.
(c) Phase 1B’s single sufficient-statistic readout is the readout of the same model.

The Subject Term: Nullspace Projection
6.1
Why a Point, Not a Cloud
The emotion branch uses a free empirical cloud matched by OT. The subject branch must not, for
three reasons:
(i) Role. The emotion cloud py is shared across subjects and is something to align to. The
subject baseline is a per-subject nuisance to capture and remove, not a prototype to match.
(ii) Degeneracy. Maximising ∑︁
t(1−αt) log ps(zt) over a free measure ps is ill-posed: the optimum
piles all mass on the single point with the largest weight.
(iii) Sufficiency and pathology. The baseline is tonic and roughly unimodal, so its first moment
is all the subject signal that matters. Keeping a rich subject cloud would reward exactly the
SCDA subject-mining pathology on the dead list.

B4. Limit behaviours: ε →0+, ε →∞, ρ →0+, ρ →1−, gate regularity.
B5. Non-degeneracy and the cmax inequality for the gate.
B6. Binary divergence: properties and fixed-point well-posedness.
B7. Boundedness and Lipschitz constants for all loss terms and Rb.
All proofs use finite-dimensional real analysis only: compactness via Heine–Borel, log x ≤x −1,
Jensen for concave log, and single-variable calculus. No measure theory, no probability, no informa-
tion theory.
