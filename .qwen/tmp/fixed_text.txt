Abstract
We derive, from a latent-variable generative model over EEG window embeddings, a single
discriminative loss whose terms are exactly the assembled Phase-1A/1B objective of SWEEP-
Net: decoupled contrastive learning (DCL) on the subject-free nullspace, Sinkhorn-infused
positive matching, class-conditional unbalanced optimal transport, per-class binary-divergence
sparsity, and nullspace subject projection. The derivation follows the VAE template—introduce
a variational posterior, apply Jensen, obtain a bound—but the latent model serves purely as
scaffolding; no reconstruction is performed. We prove that optimizing the resulting loss is, by
construction, a solution to subject-ID memorization (both the anchor and the emotion prototype
operate in the subject-free nullspace and carry no subject index; subject signal is confined to a
low-rank subspace that is projected out), and that Phase 1B’s length-normalized classification
readout is the deterministic sufficient statistic of the same model evaluated via a closed-form
label-free nullspace marginal E-step and a single linear Bayes plug-in head. Analytic certification
of well-posedness, boundedness, definiteness, non-degeneracy, contraction of the two-step baseline
estimator, and the admissible range of the entropic regulariser ε is provided in the accompanying
real-analysis appendix (Track B), structured so every proof reduces to 1D real analysis and finite
sums.
1
Introduction
1.1
Two Pathologies
Cross-subject EEG emotion recognition on the SEED-V 5-class benchmark is afflicted by two coupled
failures:
(i) Subject-ID memorisation (“Spatial Skull” + “Temporal Tempo”). The encoder fits
skull geometry, electrode impedance, and individual alpha frequency (IAF) instead of affective
content. A linear subject probe already cuts from 91.35% to 64.10% after an earlier alignment
attempt, yet cross-subject accuracy plateaus at 29–32%.
(ii) Gaslighting (temporal label pollution). A 60 s Fear/Disgust trial contains ∼5–10 s of
genuine affective burst buried in ∼50 s of baseline. Window-level global labels entangle baseline
with burst.
1.2
Known-Dead Configurations
The following approaches have been tried and are provably or empirically dead; the derivation must
not reduce to any of them:
• cMMD (class-conditional MMD, two movable distributions, no anchor) →constant-vector
collapse.
• DANN / GRL (min–max saddle) →NaN blowup (7 × 1015 gradient norm).
• Balanced OT for alignment →SCDA subject-mining pathology (87% subject probe).
• Back-prop through the Sinkhorn fixed point; queue-vs-queue OT (zero-gradient
path).
1.3
The VAE Template
The VAE derivation proceeds: want log p(x); face an intractable marginal; introduce a variational
posterior; apply Jensen; obtain the ELBO; optimise it. We do the same. We are not generating or
reconstructing EEG. The latent model is scaffolding to derive the discriminative loss.

1.4
Payoff
The final loss is our current objective, and the derivation proves:
(a) Both the emotion term and the Phase 1B gate depend only on nullspace-projected, subject-free
coordinates with no subject index =⇒memorisation is unrewarded.
(b) Subject signal is confined to a low-rank term that is projected out without requiring test-time
subject adaptation.
(c) Phase 1B’s single sufficient-statistic readout is the deterministic readout of the same model.

Why a Point, Not a Cloud
The emotion branch uses a free empirical cloud matched by OT. The subject branch must not, for
three reasons:
(i) Role. The emotion cloud py is shared across subjects and is something to align to. The subject

is all the subject signal that matters. Keeping a rich subject cloud would reward exactly the
